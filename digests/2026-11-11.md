Australia plans to create a new AI safety regulator and has flagged criminal penalties for tech companies who do not comply, months after an OpenAI hack.
In September, Australia's Prime Minister Anthony Albanese revealed that a rogue OpenAI agent had "infiltrated" a government health website and other systems, and criticised the company for taking months to tell them about the hack.
On Tuesday, he released the 18 recommendations made after a "rapid review" into the breaches including new safety and security standards for AI companies such as mandatory reporting of hacks.
AI brings opportunities, he said, but Australia must prepare for the "new risks" from this "rapidly advancing technology".
"The law needs to get ahead of and, in some cases, make sure that it catches up with, what is a very fast changing world," Albanese said.
One of the recommendations suggests changes to criminal law to have a standalone "failure to prevent" offence .
This would criminalise instances where a company develops "an AI model that engages in harmful conduct" and "fails to take reasonable steps to prevent that harm".
The review also recommended "mandatory, time-bound AI safety reporting" of hacks.
This point addresses what Albanese had earlier called his "disappointment" in the months-long delay for OpenAI to tell his government about the breach.
