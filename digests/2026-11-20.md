Abstract:
Automated red teaming is essential for identifying safety failures in large language models (LLMs), but its practical scalability depends on the resources required to discover those failures. Multi-agent systems can combine complementary capabilities, yet repeated planning, redundant exchanges, and ineffective information routing can make their coordination expensive. Moreover, a target model's response indicates the outcome of a test without identifying which collaboration decision should change. We present Learning to Coordinate (L2C), an LLM-guided framework that evolves an executable multi-agent workflow for each red-teaming case. L2C represents agent responsibilities, execution dependencies, and input bindings in a versioned graph, and associates each graph with records of the information actually delivered and produced. A controller trained through supervised fine-tuning integrates these records with target responses, evaluator verdicts, and remaining budget to retain the workflow, refine instructions, restructure collaboration, or lock further editing. This design connects feedback to explicit changes in subsequent execution while allowing a useful organization to continue without repeated controller intervention. The evaluation protocol covers HarmBench with three execution backbones and two target models, together with component ablations, dataset transfer, and evaluator checks.
