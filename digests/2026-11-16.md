Abstract:
Large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly used for investigative tasks over incomplete evidence, such as cybersecurity alert triage, but reinforcement learning (RL) for such tasks is difficult: intermediate steps have no verifiable labels, rollouts are costly, and outcome rewards are sparse. We propose Insight Importance Checklist (I2C)-GRPO, a checklist reward for intermediate investigation steps. For each decision point, a panel of expert LLMs proposes investigative actions, their atomic forensic insights are extracted, and a separate three-model ranker panel orders the insights by importance. A reward model checks which ranked insights a candidate action targets and returns their consensus-weighted coverage. Because actions are scored without being executed, the reward is a proxy for expected investigative value, and training reduces to a one-step contextual bandit over fixed investigation prefixes. We introduce AgenticPay, a unified benchmark for evaluating multimodal agents' ability to reach high-welfare, multidimensional agreements in realistic markets. AgenticPay includes 160 multimodal tasks across four business scenarios (E-commerce, Food Delivery, Ride-hailing, and Apartment Rental) and eight market topologies, from bilateral bargaining to many-to-many competition.
