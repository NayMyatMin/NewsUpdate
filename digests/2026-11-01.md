According to a paper published by Google, its AI-powered security agent has
uncovered more than 500 XSS vulnerabilities
across its web applications since entering full operation in January. Google PageBreak uses Gemini models to autonomously investigate potential vulnerabilities, but reports them only after deterministic validators successfully reproduce the exploit against a running application, resulting in a near-zero false-positive rate.
With PageBreak, every vulnerability hypothesis is tested against a running environment with a real exploit, such as executing JavaScript for XSS, writing to a world-readable location for path traversal, or triggering outbound DNS for RCE.
Michał Bentkowski
, information security engineer at Google, explains:
Early on in the development of PageBreak, we made a conscious decision to prioritize deterministic validation. Rather than simply hypothesizing bugs based on code patterns, the system closes the loop by verifying potential flaws against running environments. This approach results in a near-zero false positive rate, ensuring that we avoid overloading product teams with unverified vulnerability reports.
