Abstract:
Model quantization has become a fundamental technique for enabling the efficient deployment of large-scale deep neural networks (DNNs) in data-intensive applications by reducing model size. Despite its widespread adoption, the impact of quantization on training data privacy --- particularly vulnerability to membership inference attacks (MIAs) --- remains largely unexplored. In this paper, we present the first comprehensive empirical study of privacy risks in quantized DNNs, evaluating eight state-of-the-art MIA attacks across six representative quantization methods. Our results reveal that practical quantization levels (4-8 bits) have limited impact on membership privacy risks, with quantized models remaining largely as vulnerable as their full-precision counterparts. Although aggressive low-bit quantization (e.g., 2-bit) can provide meaningful privacy gains, these benefits come at the cost of substantial utility degradation. HoS learns how each head should redistribute attention between trusted and untrusted tokens, enabling models to reason over untrusted content without following injected instructions.
